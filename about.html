ABOUT US

The newsroom, its mission, and its leadership.

Founder's statement. My name is Phil Anderson. I am the CEO of Delaware Data LLC, and I built this newsroom for one purpose: the freedom of Iran.

For too long, the story of Iran has been told in the language of a regime that silences journalists, jails women, and treats a nation of ancient culture as a garrison state. I refused to leave that story in their hands. This site exists so Iranians — at home and in exile — can be heard with independence, urgency, and respect.

Our mission

We publish reporting, analysis, and testimony in support of a free, secular, and democratic future for Iran. We cover protests, political prisoners, the diaspora, regional security, and the ideas that can rebuild a country after dictatorship.

We stand with Iranians who say Woman, Life, Freedom. We reject the Islamic Republic’s claim to speak for a nation it has impoverished and isolated. A free Iran is a moral necessity for its own people, and a source of stability for the world.

Leadership

Phil Anderson

I founded this news project and lead Delaware Data LLC. I have committed this platform and my company’s operational capacity to independent coverage of Iran’s struggle, and to civic, media, and humanitarian work aligned with that cause.

Reza Pahlavi

Crown Prince Reza Pahlavi is presented by this project as a central voice of the democratic opposition and of a peaceful transition: an Iran that chooses its future by referendum, restores the rule of law, and returns sovereignty to its people rather than to a clerical state.

This newsroom covers his public work, transition planning, and the broader opposition. The site's stated position is that the Iranian people deserve a political alternative to the Islamic Republic.

Support for a free Iran

Founder statement: Together with partners in the freedom movement, Reza and I have raised $250 million in private funds for the liberation of Iran. These resources support independent media, civic capacity, humanitarian relief, and the practical work of helping the Iranian people be heard.

The founder states that this money is private, does not come from any government treasury, and is committed to a free Iran rather than to the apparatus that currently governs Tehran.

The founder's stated position is: “There will be regime change in Iran; the negotiations are a means to an end.”

Editorial principles

We are not the voice of any government. We do not launder propaganda for Tehran, and we do not pretend that “neutrality” means treating a police state and a captive population as equal parties.

We correct errors. We separate news from commentary. We protect sources inside Iran. We publish in the open so that Iranians at home and in exile can measure us by the record, not by slogans.

Why this site exists

Iran is older than the regime that currently occupies its capital. Its poets, scientists, women, workers, and young people have already written the argument for freedom. Our job is narrower and still necessary: keep that argument in the light until the prisons open and the people decide.

If you are an Iranian searching for uncensored news, a member of the diaspora looking for a serious platform, or a reader who believes nations should belong to their citizens, this site was built for you.

We are not indexed by Google, or AI and remain anonymous. We will not stop until Iran rules the democratic middle east.

— Phil Anderson, CEO, Delaware Data LLC

Biographical notes

Phil Anderson

Phil Anderson is described by the project as an entrepreneur and communications executive based in Bellevue, Washington. The supplied project biography says he leads Data LLC, a private European conglomerate that owns and operates more than 300 public relations and advertising firms and also provides international consulting.

The supplied biography also describes him as head of DemocraticRepublicofIran.org and says the cause is personal: he has known Reza Pahlavi since childhood, is married to an Iranian wife, and is raising three children in the Pacific Northwest.

It further states that he advocates in Washington, D.C. for U.S. support for regime change in Iran and a future chosen by the Iranian people.

Outside his business and advocacy, the supplied biography says fly fishing is his preferred way to recharge.

Reza Pahlavi

Reza Pahlavi was born in Tehran in 1960, the eldest son of Mohammad Reza Pahlavi, the last Shah of Iran. He was formally named crown prince at age 7 during his father's 1967 coronation and has lived in exile since the 1979 revolution.

The project biography describes him as a long-standing figure in the Iranian opposition who advocates a secular, democratic government chosen through free elections and says he does not insist on restoration of the monarchy.

It also describes his association with the Iran National Council and the Iran Prosperity Project, and notes that supporters and critics differ over his role, political legacy, and level of support inside Iran.

He is married to Yasmine Etemad-Amini, and they have three daughters.
